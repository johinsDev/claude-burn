Where the money actually went

Measured across two months of real usage — 35,842 billed turns, priced at list rates.

  • 67% cache_read — re-reading context every turn
  • 24% cache_write
  • 7.6% output — what Claude actually writes
  • 0.03% fresh input

You are paying, over and over, to re-send a conversation that already happened.

Three things that change the number

Each one was found by reading the transcripts, not by guessing. Other tools miss all three.

$385

Subagents are billed separately

Their turns don't live in the session transcript — they're in <session>/subagents/agent-*.jsonl. Anything reading only the top level ignores them. In one real measurement: 304 agents across 51 sessions. One session spent 38% of its cost there.

33,241

Resuming a session double-counts

Resuming replays earlier lines into a brand-new file. Without deduplicating on requestId those turns are billed to you twice in the report. That was 33,241 duplicates across 35,842 turns — nearly one in two.

≈

Not every account bills per token

A subscription with overage disabled charges you a flat rate no matter what. Adding it to the total inflates your bill with money nobody charges you. claude-burn reads hasExtraUsageEnabled, marks those with ≈, and excludes them from every budget.

the part that isn't a dashboard

It can stop you.

Every other tool tells you after the fact. claude-burn ships a UserPromptSubmit hook that cuts the turn before it's sent once you're over your cap.

You turn it on and choose which caps it enforces from the app, not by editing shell. That's deliberate: while the block is active, the message you'd use to ask Claude to lift it gets blocked too. The way out can't live inside the chat.

It reads the local database in ~17 ms instead of shelling out to npx ccusage on every prompt — 100× faster, no stale cache, and it counts the subagents.

What's in it

Menu bar meter

Today's billable spend and how much of your daily cap that is. Always visible, never a surprise.

Daily · weekly · monthly caps

Calendar week and month, matching how Anthropic bills. A rolling 30-day window never resets, so it can't work as a cap.

Projection

Where the month lands at your current pace, and what you can still spend per day for the days that remain.

Four alerts

Budget steps, context bloat on a live session, plan limits, and expensive-model share — with cooldown keyed to the step reached.

Multi-account

Auto-discovers every ~/.claude* config dir. Add, hide or remove them. Each is classified as overage, flat rate, or unknown — never guessed.

Model breakdown

Including the counterfactual: what the same work would have cost on a cheaper model. Premium models are derived from the price table, not a hardcoded list.

Session titles & search

Read from the ai-title lines already in your transcripts. 122 of 123 sessions identified in a real dataset.

Cleanup

Delete old subagent transcripts by age. Safe for your numbers — turns are already deduplicated in SQLite and every query reads from there.

CLI

The same engine on the command line. burn-cli sessions, agents, composition, status — the last one is what the hooks read.

It never phones home

Your transcripts contain your source code. So claude-burn makes zero network requests — not to Anthropic's API, not to telemetry, not to anything. It reads files Claude Code already wrote to disk and keeps a SQLite database on your machine.

  • Never reads .credentials.json
  • Plan limits come from cachedUsageUtilization, which Claude Code refreshes itself
  • No account, no sign-in, no analytics
  • MIT licensed — read every line
